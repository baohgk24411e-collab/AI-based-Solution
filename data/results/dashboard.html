## Pipeline Execution Stages
1. **Document Ingestion:** Reads 20 official English PDF reports from Nestlé, Danone, Coca-Cola, P&G, and Reckitt.
2. **Chunking & Indexing:** Tokenizes and indexes text into 4,930 searchable passages with exact page tracking.
3. **Keyword Filtering:** Filters 2,565 total pages down to 389 packaging-relevant candidate pages to conserve compute.
4. **LLM Claim Extraction:** Prompts the model with strict JSON schema to parse:
   * `claim_text`: Exact verbatim quotation from the text.
   * `claim_type`: `target`, `recyclability`, `recycled_content`, `collection_recycling`.
   * Quantitative metadata: `value`, `unit`, `target_year`, `measurement_year`.
5. **Exact Substring Gatekeeper:** Validates that `claim_text` exists literally on the cited PDF page. Any fabricated or altered quotation is immediately discarded.
6. **BM25 Evidence Retrieval:** Performs keyword-driven document retrieval for each claim across the entire publication to retrieve corresponding disclosures, definitions, or conflicting footnotes.
7. **Adjudication & Verification:** Compares the claim against evidence chunks, producing labels:
   * `supported`: Substantive corroborating evidence found in the document.
   * `insufficient_evidence`: Broad statements or metrics lacking methodology or baseline context.
   * `scope_mismatch`: Plastic claims mixed with paper, glass, or non-packaging scopes.
   * `potential_inconsistency`: Conflicting figures across different sections or years.
   * `not_yet_assessable`: Future commitments that have not matured.

Engineering Limitations & System Constraints

# Pipeline Limitations & Engineering Constraints

## 1. PDF Text Extraction & Structural Layout Boundaries
* **Pure Text Stream (No OCR):** The ingestion pipeline relies on `pypdf` stream extraction. Documents containing scanned images, rasterized text, or complex infographic banners without text layers cannot be extracted without an OCR subsystem (e.g., Tesseract/PaddleOCR).
* **Multi-Column Tables & Layout Disruption:** Sustainability reports often present metrics in multi-column tables. Linear PDF text extraction can flatten rows into ambiguous sequences, occasionally causing LLMs to associate metric values with adjacent row headers or neighboring years.
* **Page Indexing vs. Printed Folios:** Physical PDF page indices (1-indexed) frequently diverge from printed publication folios due to front-matter, cover pages, and Roman numeral numbering.

## 2. Evidence Retrieval (BM25 Lexical Scope)
* **Intra-Report Retrieval:** Current evidence retrieval is strictly scoped within the same report ID. Cross-year comparative analysis (e.g., comparing 2021 baseline vs. 2024 claimed achievement across different annual publications) is not autonomously linked in this single-report retrieval pass.
* **Lexical vs. Semantic Embedding:** BM25 matching prioritizes keyword frequencies. Claims utilizing heavy paraphrase or corporate euphemisms without overlapping packaging keywords may experience lower retrieval recall than dense semantic vector embeddings.

## 3. LLM Extraction & Assessment Hallucination Patterns
* **Neighboring Table Cell Confusion (12.7% Metric Error Rate):** When extracting quantitative fields (`value`, `unit`, `target_year`), small local models (e.g., 7B parameters) occasionally pick up progress percentages from adjacent table rows rather than the target percentage stated in the sentence.
* **Implicit Verbal Deduction:** Phrases like *"halve our virgin plastics"* are occasionally converted into numeric `50%` by the LLM even though the digit `50` does not literally appear in the sentence.
* **Documentary Support vs. Factual Truth:** A verdict of `supported` exclusively denotes that the cited report text contains matching claims and documentary substantiation. It does **not** constitute independent forensic auditing or external physical verification of corporate practices.

## 4. Greenwashing Detection Scope
* **Report-Internal Consistency:** The system flags internal discrepancies (e.g., future commitments presented as achieved, lack of baseline years, scope mismatch between plastic vs. all packaging). It cannot detect supply-chain fraud, unrecorded waste exports, or off-balance-sheet environmental impacts occurring outside official corporate filings.

Greenwashing Taxonomy Framework

# Greenwashing Classification Taxonomy in Plastic Packaging

## Framework Overview
In corporate sustainability disclosures, "Greenwashing" refers to practices that mislead consumers, investors, or regulators regarding an enterprise's environmental impact or the sustainability performance of its products.

In our analysis of FMCG packaging reports, we identify 5 primary risk patterns:

### 1. The Sin of No Proof (Insufficient Evidence)
* **Definition:** Claims asserting high sustainability or packaging reduction without transparent measurement methodologies, baseline years, or third-party audit verification.
* **Corpus Example:** Statements such as *"our refill packs are designed to be fully recyclable"* without reporting actual collection rates, infrastructure compatibility, or consumer recycling rates.

### 2. The Sin of Vagueness (Unqualified Scope)
* **Definition:** Using broad buzzwords such as *"sustainable packaging"*, *"eco-friendly design"*, or *"circular plastic"* without specifying whether the scope is global, regional, virgin plastic only, or total packaging mass.
* **Corpus Example:** Reporting composite percentages (e.g., *"88% recyclable, reusable, or compostable packaging"*) to mask that actual post-consumer recycled plastic content remains in single digits.

### 3. False Equivalence & Scope Mismatch
* **Definition:** Blending distinct packaging substrates (e.g., glass, aluminum, corrugated cardboard, and PET) into an aggregated "sustainable packaging" metric to inflate the headline percentage while high-risk virgin plastic usage expands.
* **Corpus Example:** Advertising a high headline packaging recyclability rate driven primarily by heavy glass bottles and corrugated shipping boxes, while flexible plastic pouches remain non-recyclable.

### 4. Premature Achievement Claims (Not Yet Assessable)
* **Definition:** Framing distant future commitments (e.g., *"100% recyclable by 2025/2030"*) with promotional marketing rhetoric in the present tense, conflating target aspirations with achieved operating performance.
* **Corpus Example:** Presenting headline reduction targets without annual progress baselines or interim milestones.

### 5. Hidden Trade-Offs & Selective Disclosure
* **Definition:** Highlighting an isolated environmental gain (such as a 10% weight reduction in bottle caps) while total virgin resin tonnage increases due to aggregate production volume expansion.
